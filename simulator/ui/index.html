    About

    What you are looking at

    Replay draws the India Meteorological Department's 0.25° daily rainfall grid (Pai et al. 2014), every day 1998–2022. The 2,759 CGWB monitoring wells are coloured by how far their water level moved at the last quarterly reading.

    Scenario runs the simulator model, a bidirectional LSTM, live in your browser. It reads the 104 weeks of rain before a reading, plus fixed facts about the well: depth, specific yield, aquifer and normal rainfall. It predicts the change in water level, and never sees a past water level, a year or a location. The rain slider rescales those 104 weeks and runs it again.

    Pressure is the project's question: where the water fell further than the rain explains. That gap is a proxy for extraction, not a measurement of it.

    Limits, stated plainly

    • The model's rain response is small: centimetres to tens of centimetres across the whole slider. Groundwater moves slowly, and pumping dominates in many districts.
    • It was trained with a penalty so that more rain cannot predict a larger fall. The penalty is soft, so the share of wells that still move the wrong way is shown under the scenario.
    • Dams are from GeoDAR v1.1: about 1,300 in India, a fifth of the national register, shown for context. Nothing here models reservoirs or floods.
    • Rivers are HydroSHEDS HydroRIVERS reaches with a mean flow above 15 m³/s. Relief is from AWS Terrain Tiles.
    • Boundaries are the post-2020 district outlines, with India as depicted by the Survey of India.
