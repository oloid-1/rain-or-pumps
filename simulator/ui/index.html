    Details
    How this works

    The model is re-run for every well reading the extra rain can reach, using the rain that actually fell around those days (or, for future dates, rain like each past year in turn, which gives the range). It knows the season: 40 mm in a dry month counts for more than 40 mm in the monsoon. Changes of a few centimetres are within the model's noise.

    How reliable is this?

    The water level is stepped forward from the last reading in November 2022, one quarter at a time, with rain like each past year in turn. Tested on 2015–2022, this beat “assume no change” up to 5 years ahead, but not beyond.

    “If recent pumping continues” adds each district's 2015–22 fall that rain did not explain (mostly pumping, also recharge works and canals). It is an assumption, not a prediction.

    Pumping hotspots

    Where did water fall more than rain explains?

    Orange districts lost more water than the rain there accounts for: the likely sign of pumping. Blue districts did better than rain alone.

    Which years?

    Before 2015 the model learned from these years, so the gaps it shows there come out smaller than they really are. Compare those years with each other, not with 2015 onwards.

      For each district: the change actually measured in its wells, minus the change the model expects from rain alone. It points to where to look; it does not measure pumping. Only 2015–2022 is shown because the model learned from the years before. A district needs at least two monitored wells.
