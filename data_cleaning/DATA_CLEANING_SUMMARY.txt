==========================================================================================
1. GOAL AND SCOPE
==========================================================================================

Project question: given the rain that fell, where should the groundwater be, and how much of
the decline does rain NOT explain (the pressure from pumping)? That needs two very different
datasets joined correctly:

  groundwater  point measurements at wells, four times a year, depth in metres
  rainfall     a daily grid of 0.25-degree cells, millimetres per day

Reviewer feedback asked for proper data cleaning and data building on both. This work does
both: it cleans each dataset on its own terms, then aligns them in space (same places), time
(same periods) and unit (same quantity).

Scope decisions:
  - Years: rain 1998-2022, water levels 2000-2022. The raw file also has 2023-24 readings
    (53,380); they are kept but not used, because there is no rain data for them (decision).
  - The earlier model pipeline is set aside and archived in old/ (decision). The new data
    is the baseline for all future models.

4. GROUNDWATER (CGWB) CLEANING
==========================================================================================

4.1  The file we had been using was already filtered, and the filter was biased
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Finding. Replaying the source paper's quality control on the raw file reproduces its 2,759
wells exactly, which shows where the other 29,540 went:

    raw file                                                   32,299 wells
    has any reading 2000-22                                    29,537
    no negative reading anywhere (drops the whole well)        28,612
    AT LEAST TWO READINGS IN EVERY YEAR 2000-22                 2,876
    no value repeating more than twice in a row                 2,759

The fourth rule alone removes 25,736 wells. It tests completeness, not quality: a well with
91 of 92 readings is dropped if one year has a single reading. The dropped wells deepen more
often: 46% of the wells the rebuild adds are deepening, against 38% of the published ones.
So the published set understates the decline.

Change. Start from the raw file; drop nothing; flag problems instead (4.2); choose wells only
at the end, as named "views":

    view_strict      the published 2,759 wells (control group)        2,759 wells
    view_modelling   a usable normal in all four campaigns             8,629 wells
    view_training    a usable normal in three of the four (see 10)    11,455 wells

("usable normal" = at least 5 valid readings in the training years 2000-2014.)

4.2  Bad readings: flagged, never deleted
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Each reading gets six flags. Flagged readings stay in well_obs; everything built on top uses
only valid ones. Counts (data/qc_audit.csv):

    flag                meaning                                        readings    wells
    ------------------  ---------------------------------------------  ---------  -------
    flag_negative       water above ground (flowing well or sign error)    1,429      925
    flag_zero           exactly 0.000 m (NOT invalid on its own)          15,062    1,831
    flag_zero_suspect   a zero in a well whose zeros are over 1/5 of its  13,488      753
                        record: missing data written as 0
    flag_below_bottom   water deeper than the well was drilled            26,257    2,755
    flag_outlier        over 3 sigma from the well's OWN seasonal         9,587    7,485
                        pattern (so a real long decline is not clipped)
    flag_repeat_run     3+ identical readings in a row                    22,448    1,950
    ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
    invalid (any of the above except a plain zero)                        59,977   11,426
    valid                                                              1,065,392   31,356
                                                                      (94.7% of 1,125,369)

A plain zero is not invalid: shallow wells can fill to the surface in the monsoon.
Bad data is concentrated: Telangana has 16.7% suspect zeros and 17.9% below-bottom readings,
against 0.2% and 0.8% for dug wells nationally; bore wells and piezometers hold nearly all.

4.3  Identity and coordinates
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Finding. The station code was damaged by scientific notation (e.g. 1.4113E+14), so it
cannot identify a well. 3,710 wells share a coordinate with another; 2,999 have coordinates
rounded to a coarse grid.
Change. Each well is keyed by its coordinates (well_uid), with a stable suffix where wells
share a site. Rounded wells are flagged (coord_rounded) and their rain is averaged over the
area their true position could be in. 3,253 wells have no usable drilled depth
(depth_missing), so the below-bottom check skips them.

4.4  Location from coordinates, not from district names
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Finding. 610 district names mix spellings (Rajnadgaon/Rajnandgaon, Ranga reddy/Rangareddy,
Jhunjhunu/Jhunjhunun, Viluppuram/Villupuram, ...), old and new names (Trichur/Thrissur,
Belgaum/Belagavi) and boundary vintages (Telangana's 10 districts before 2016 beside its 33
after). Names cannot be joined reliably to anything.
Change. Each well is placed in the district outline its coordinates fall in; that becomes
its district and state. The file's labels are kept as cgwb_district / cgwb_state.

    placed inside an outline                                   32,205 wells
    just off a coast or border, nearest outline within 5 km        87
    more than 5 km from every outline (no district)                  7

The file's district name agrees with the outline for 82.5% of wells; nearly all the rest
are renames and splits, which is exactly what placing by coordinates fixes.

4.5  State labels that are wrong
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Finding. 489 wells stand in a different state from the one the file gives (mostly wells
filed under Andhra Pradesh standing in Maharashtra, Rajasthan, Gujarat). Either the label or
the coordinates must be wrong.
Test. A well's level should move with its neighbours. For the 262 such wells with enough
readings, the level correlates with wells at its coordinates at a median 0.39, and with
wells of its labelled district at 0.17; the coordinates fit better for 69%.
Change. The label is taken as wrong; the wells stay where their coordinates put them;
state_agrees = False records the disagreement. Andhra Pradesh and Telangana count as one
state (2014 split).

4.6  Readings have a month but no day
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Every reading is placed on the 15th of its month (Jan, May, Aug, Nov). The true day is
unknown, so season boundaries are uncertain by about +/- 2 weeks.

4.7  Some survey rounds barely happened
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Share of modelling wells read: May 2020 1%, May 2021 1%, Aug 2012 3%, Jan 2016 12%,
Aug 2020 41%. Nothing is filled in; changes bridge the gap explicitly (7.3).


==========================================================================================
5. RAINFALL (IMD) CLEANING
==========================================================================================

5.1  -999 outside land
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
12,451 of the 17,415 grid points are coded -999 (sea, outside India). Read as missing, never
as 0 mm: a missing value summed as zero would invent a dry day. 4,964 land cells remain,
the same in every year (checked).

5.2  Structural checks of the raw grid
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
All 25 files: every day of the year present (365/366), same grid layout, no negative values.
Largest daily value 979 mm (2022, north-east): consistent with real extremes there; kept.

5.3  Zeros that are really missing data  (found during the rain report; fixed)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Finding. Besides -999, IMD writes exactly 0.0 mm on every day of a year in some land cells
with no rain gauge nearby: 669 cell-years in 109 cells, mostly the edges of the grid
(Arunachal Pradesh 57 cells, Gujarat 14, Mizoram 11, Manipur 6, Rajasthan 4, Haryana 3,
West Bengal 1). A further 665 monsoon cell-months read 0 where that month's median is over
100 mm. Example: Saiha district (Mizoram) read 0 mm in 1998-2006, 2011 and 2012, and
2,000-3,000 mm in other years, which produced a false +1,296 mm/decade "trend".
Change.
  - rain_panel flags these cell-months suspect_zero (8,693 in total) and sets them missing.
  - seasons blanks their days, so a season touching one is incomplete, not dry.
  - every average over cells (a well's four cells, a district's cells) now uses only cells
    with data and renormalises their weights; rain_cover records the share that had data.
Effect. Saiha: 2,538 mm a year with 31% variability (was 1,402 mm and 100%). 26 districts
and 30 modelling wells were affected. 189 well-months have no rain left (every cell around
the well was flagged) and stay missing.

5.4  Rain at a well: interpolation, not the nearest cell
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A well rarely sits on a grid point, and one near a cell edge gets most of its rain from the
neighbour. Rain at a well is interpolated from the four surrounding points, with weights
renormalised over land points (bridge_well_cell). Tested by hiding grid points and
predicting them back: monthly error 17.7% against 25.6% for the nearest point. Wet days and
the wettest day are counted at the grid points first, then interpolated (bias +0.2% and
-0.7%, against +12% and -12% the other way round).


==========================================================================================
6. PUTTING BOTH ON THE SAME MAP
==========================================================================================

6.1  Grid cell -> district -> state, by area
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Each grid point is the centre of a 0.25 x 0.25 degree box (about 28 km a side). Each box is
cut by the district outlines; the overlap in km2 is kept (bridge_cell_district):

    area km2           = overlap in degrees2 x 111.2^2 x cos(latitude)
    frac_of_cell       = overlap / box
    weight_in_district = overlap / the district's total gridded land

District rain = sum over its cells of (cell rain x weight_in_district). State rain uses the
same overlaps summed by state. Results:

    cell x district pairs            10,272
    land cells mapped                 4,873 of 4,964 (91 lie beyond the drawn border)
    districts with rain                 720 of 724 (the 4 missed are islands)
    cells split across districts      3,449;  across states 950
    gridded land                       3.25 million km2 (India's official area 3.29)

Before this, a district's rain was averaged over the cells its wells stood in: districts
without wells had no rain (702 reached) and shared cells counted in full for each district.

6.2  Evidence (scripts/rain_grid_mapping.py -> data/report/rain_grid_mapping.txt)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Rebuilt from scratch and checked: identical to the stored table; weights sum to 1 in every
district; median gridded/outline area ratio 1.000. Worked example, Pune, May-Aug 2019,
computed by hand from the raw NetCDF: 903.353 mm, matching the table to 0.00003 mm. A plain
mean of Pune's cells would give 1,182.6 mm, because 16 of its 36 cells lie mostly outside it.
One flaw in the outline file is recorded: the new Vav-Tharad district overlaps its parent
Banaskantha in 9 cells by a few percent; left as drawn.


==========================================================================================
7. PUTTING BOTH ON THE SAME CLOCK: FOUR SEASONS
==========================================================================================

7.1  Why not months or calendar quarters
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Rain is daily; levels are read four times a year. A Jul-Sep quarter would straddle the
August reading and mix rain the level has already answered with rain it has not. So a
season runs from the day after one reading to the next reading, cut on the 15th:

    season    from     to       days     closed by          label
    Nov-Jan   Nov 16   Jan 15    61      January reading    post-monsoon
    Jan-May   Jan 16   May 15   120/121  May reading        dry
    May-Aug   May 16   Aug 15    92      August reading     early monsoon
    Aug-Nov   Aug 16   Nov 15    92      November reading   late monsoon

99 seasons in all, Jan-May 1998 to Aug-Nov 2022 (only seasons fully inside the rain record).

7.2  Season rain is summed from the daily grid
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The cut falls mid-month, so no monthly table can produce these totals. Checked against the
raw NetCDF for sample cells and seasons (including one crossing a year boundary and a leap
year): maximum difference 0.00004 mm.

7.3  A missing reading is bridged, never filled
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The change is taken from the last valid reading, up to one year back; span_seasons says how
far; rain is summed over the whole span to match. In the modelling set 88% of changes come
from the season just before.


==========================================================================================
8. PUTTING BOTH IN THE SAME UNIT
==========================================================================================

A water level is a stock (how full the aquifer is at one moment); rain is a flow (water
arriving over a period). Rain in a season explains the CHANGE in level over that season, not
the level. Specific yield (Sy, the share of the rock that holds water) converts the change:

    delta_h_m          = depth now - depth at previous reading      (+ = water fell)
    storage_change_mm  = -delta_h_m x Sy x 1000                     (+ = water gained)
    recharge_ratio     = storage_change_mm / rain over the same span  (only if >= 25 mm)

Median result, modelling wells:

    season    level change    rain     share of rain stored
    Nov-Jan   falls 0.65 m      7 mm   -
    Jan-May   falls 1.42 m     39 mm   -
    May-Aug   RISES 1.94 m    482 mm   +11.6%
    Aug-Nov   falls 0.15 m    359 mm   about 0

The May-Aug figure sits inside CGWB's own published infiltration range (8-15%). Outside the
monsoon the ratio is a net loss per mm of rain, not recharge.

9. SECOND PASS: GAPS CLOSED BY clean_gaps.ipynb
==========================================================================================

9.1  Specific yield from the hydrogeological map
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Finding. The raw file has no Sy. The first pass filled 29,419 wells with the median of their
state and aquifer type (a guess). The map in the archive holds five classes (0.018 folded
metasediments/aquitard, 0.020 jointed crystalline, 0.023 fractured crystalline, 0.043
consolidated sedimentary, 0.130 alluvium).
Test. Sampled at the published wells, the map returns the published value for 100% of the
2,872 that fall on a map cell. Against the map, the guesses were a different class for
8,996 wells, and for 75% of those off by more than 1.5x.
Change. Every well takes its Sy from the map: 29,307 directly, 112 coastal wells from the
nearest map cell within two cells; published values unchanged (2,880). All modelling wells
now have a map-based Sy class; rock_class names the rock. Old values kept as sy_ingest.

9.2  Aquifer type and well type
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Finding. aquifer is "-" or "Not available" for 13,312 wells (1,697 modelling); well_type is
"-" for 2,239. Of dug wells with a recorded aquifer type, 99.6% are unconfined (13,912 of
13,964); dug-cum-bore 100% (17 of 17). Bore (64%), tube (80%), piezometer (77%) are mixed.
Change. Blank dug wells -> Unconfined (6,308 wells, 1,229 modelling). Other blanks ->
Unknown (7,004 wells, 468 modelling); blank well types -> Unknown. aquifer_source records
cgwb / inferred_dug_well / unknown; originals kept as aquifer_cgwb, well_type_cgwb.
Aquifer type now known or inferred for 94.6% of modelling wells.

9.3  Rebuilt the tables that carry Sy and aquifer; built fact_cell_season_full (rain per
     cell per season with the wells in the cell); refreshed the data dictionary. Assertions
     at the end of the notebook check all of it; running it twice gives the same result.


==========================================================================================
10. TRAINING DATA FOR THE MODELS
==========================================================================================

10.1  A suggested modelling approach was tested first (MODELLING_GAPS.md section 1)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Suggested: an RNN reading 30 days of rain to predict rain at the well on day 31, feeding its
own predictions back. Tested on 300 random cells, 2018-22: the last 30 days predict the next
day at R2 0.145, worse than the calendar average (0.17); fed back, skill falls to the
average within a week and the forecast's spread shrinks to a third of reality. Rain at the
wells is already observed, so it is not a target. Kept from the suggestion: distance
weighting, the error cost, "rain in -> groundwater out", and comparing two architectures.

10.2  Four states restored
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Finding. Requiring all four readings excluded every well in Kerala (0 of 1,947) and Assam
(0 of 478) and all but 4 in West Bengal and Odisha: their raw record has no May reading.
Change. view_training needs three of four: 11,455 wells in 27 states and 598 districts;
Kerala 703, West Bengal 701, Odisha 837, Assam 211. Their Jan -> Aug change spans two
seasons, with rain summed to match.

10.3  What was built (data/training/)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
    tabular.parquet     694,253 well-season rows with a target
                        train 2000-2014: 489,707 | valid 2015-2017: 85,250 |
                        test 2018-2022: 119,296; plus 5 folds of whole districts
    features (25)       this season's rain, rain since the previous reading, span, % of
                        normal, wet days, wettest day, normal, rain_cover, season length;
                        rain 1-3 seasons before; rain over the last 4, 8, 12 seasons and each
                        as % of normal; Sy, drilled depth, normal annual rain; categories:
                        season, aquifer, well type, rock class
    target              delta_h_m (alternatives: storage_change_mm, anomaly_ref_m)
    sequence_cells.npz  weekly rain for the 52 weeks up to each reading, per land cell
                        (99 x 52 x 4,964), 51 MB; stencil.parquet + a loader build any
                        well's sequence (for a recurrent model)
    feature_spec.csv    role of every column (feature, target, id, excluded, ...)

10.4  Leakage guards
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Never inputs: any past water level, the year, coordinates, district, state. The build stops
if one is listed as a feature. Rain and level normals come from 2000-2014 only.

==========================================================================================
13. DECISIONS TAKEN, AND WHY
==========================================================================================

  - Flag, don't drop. Any rule can be changed later without re-ingesting.
  - Coordinates over names for location; tested on the 489 conflicting wells (4.5).
  - Readings on the 15th; seasons between readings, not calendar quarters (7.1).
  - Rain pairs with the change in level, not the level (8).
  - Missing is missing: no reading, rain value or season is ever filled in.
  - 2023-24 out of scope: no rain for those years.
  - The old pipeline archived in old/; the new data is the baseline.
  - Rain is an input, never a target: it is observed, and cannot be forecast from its own
    past (10.1). "Future" groundwater will be asked with scenario rain (e.g. +20%).
  - Three of four campaigns for training, to keep Kerala, Assam, West Bengal and Odisha.

15. HOW TO REBUILD EVERYTHING
==========================================================================================

From ml/, with the raw files in data/:

    python -m bits_ml.ingest
    python -m bits_ml.grid
    python -m bits_ml.rain_panel
    python -m bits_ml.panel
    python -m bits_ml.seasons
    (run data_cleaning/clean_gaps.ipynb top to bottom)
    python -m scripts.core_report
    python -m scripts.rain_grid_mapping
    python -m scripts.rain_report

The notebook must be re-run after any re-run of ingest (which rewrites well_master).
